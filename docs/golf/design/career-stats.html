§ 1What it is

The career view, next to Trends and Rounds

§29 put both Trends and Rounds behind one chooser: History → pick a player → Trends / Rounds. Stats is a third row on that chooser, opening a new GolfCareerStatsActivity(slot, playerName). Back returns to the chooser, like the other two.

§ 2What it shows

Five things, in priority order

1 — Handicap index (the headline)

WHS-style: the average of the lowest 8 of your last 20 score differentials, times 0.96. With fewer than 20 rounds a sliding rule applies (3 rounds → lowest 1 minus 2.0; 6 → lowest 2; … up to 20). Under 3 rounds it reads “need 3 rounds.” See §4 for the differential.

2 — Score shape

Career counts and percentages for eagle-or-better / birdie / par / bogey / double / triple-plus, with a bar. The single most legible “this is my game” picture.

3 — Scoring by par type

Average score, and average to-par, on your par 3s, par 4s, par 5s. Shows where the shots leak.

4 — Around the green

Scrambling — of the greens you missed, the share you still made par or better (uses the GIR bit from §31). Plus putts per GIR and 3-putt %. If bunker tracking is added (§6), sand-save % joins here.

5 — Records

Lowest round (and where), lowest 9, fewest putts, most pars in a round, longest bogey-free run. Small, but it's what people screenshot.

§ 3Where the numbers come from

Score shape and par-type scoring need per-hole data

index.csv stores per-round totals only. Handicap, lowest round, fewest putts — those fold straight over the index like Trends does. But score shape, par-type scoring and scrambling need each hole's score against its par, which the index doesn't carry. Three ways to get it:

OptionHowCost
A — open the round files (recommended for v1) On entering the screen, stream every archived round JSON once, tally, show. A short “Reading 24 rounds…” frame while it works. ~1–3 s load, occasional screen. No format change. Simplest thing that ships.
B — a career-stats cache A per-player /golf/rounds/career-<slot>.bin updated on each archive, rebuilt from round files if missing/stale. Instant load. A new derived file + invalidation logic. The optimisation path if A ever feels slow.
C — widen index.csv to v6 ~13 more columns per row (the score histogram, par-type sums, scramble counts, the differential), computed at archive, backfilled on rebuild. Pure fold, fast. But it's a format bump right after v5, and the row is already 17 columns wide. Rejected for now.

Recommendation: A, then B if needed

Career stats is a screen you open now and then, not every hole. A 2-second read of the round files is a fair trade for zero format churn. If it drags, B slots in underneath with nothing user-facing changing. The handicap differential is the one value worth precomputing — store it as a single differential column so the handicap number is instant even under A.

§ 4The handicap number

Course rating and slope are course data, not user input

A score differential is (adjusted gross − course rating) × 113 ÷ slope. That needs two numbers per tee that the course files don't have yet: 

{
  "name": "Sanyang Golf Club",
  "tees": "Blue",
  "rating": 71.2,          // NEW -- course rating for this tee, one decimal
  "slope": 128,            // NEW -- slope, 55..155 (default 113 if absent)
  "par":   [...], "yards": [...], "si": [...]
}
  • Both optional. A round on a tee with no rating is simply left out of the handicap (and the screen says so) — every other stat still counts it.
  • Adjusted gross caps each hole at net double bogey (par + 2 + handicap strokes received). Needs per-hole scores — option A gives them.
  • Handicap index = mean of the lowest N of the last 20 differentials × 0.96, where N follows the WHS table (3→1, 4→1, 5→1, 6→2, … 20→8), with the −2.0 / −1.0 adjustment at the low end.

Decision: handicap in v1, or v2?

Handicap is the headline, but it's the only part that needs new course data (rating/slope on every tee file) and the net-double-bogey cap. If you'd rather ship §2's items 2–5 first and add handicap once the course files carry ratings, the screen works fine without it — the hero slot just shows scoring average instead.

§ 5The screen

480 × 800, 1-bit, one scroll

Rows past the fold reached with the side rocker, wrapping, like every other golf list. If a section has no data yet (no par-5s played, no ratings) its rows read “—” rather than vanishing, so the layout is stable.
