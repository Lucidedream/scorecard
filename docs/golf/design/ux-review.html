Review · Scorecard · End to end

Four findings, one of them worth building

A screen-by-screen pass over everything except hole-by-hole scoring. Most of the app holds up. What it lacks is an ending — and underneath that, three consistency defects that are cheap to fix and currently visible on every screen.

1 Finishing a round tells you nothing. The screen that would fix it already exists. Build this
2 Four screens print the same label on two footer cells. Defect
3 Three vocabularies for one gesture; two words for one action. Consistency
4 Duplicate i18n keys for strings upstream already defines. Tidy
§ 1The missing ending

You finish a round and the app says nothing

Press Finish round, confirm, and GolfRoundMenuActivity calls openGolfHome(). You land on the main menu. No score, no to-par, no acknowledgement that four hours of golf just happened.

This is the one place where the app is meaningfully worse than the paper card it replaces. On paper you total the columns and see the number. Here the number exists, is already computed, and is never shown at the moment you most want it.

The screen is already built

GolfRoundSummaryActivity renders score, to-par, putts, the bucket split and penalties. It is currently reachable only through History → player → round → summary — four presses after the fact, for a screen whose natural moment is the second the round ends.

Showing it on finish is a routing change, not a new screen. Back from it goes to the main menu exactly as today, so nothing else moves.

Why this is the top finding

  • It is the highest-value change per line of code in the whole review — one navigation call against a screen that already exists and is already tested.
  • It closes the loop. Every other flow in the app ends by telling you something; this one ends by dropping you at a menu.
  • It makes the round feel finished, which is the actual product being built.
§ 2Duplicate labels

Two buttons, one word, four screens

Sweeping every mapLabels() call turned up four screens that print the same label on two cells. A footer's whole job is to say what each button does; repeating a word means one of the two is unexplained.

Scorecard (card)Back Next tabPrev tabNext tab
StatisticsBack Back——
TrendsBack Back——
Round summaryBack Back——

On the card, Confirm and Right both say "Next tab" and both do the same thing, so Confirm is a redundant control wearing a label that implies otherwise. On the other three, Confirm duplicates Back.

The underlying intent is fine — letting either button exit is forgiving. The fix is to stop advertising the duplicate: leave Confirm blank where it merely mirrors Back, and on the card either blank it or give it a distinct job.

Worth checking before changing the card

Blanking Confirm on the card removes a way to change tabs. If you have been using Confirm rather than Right for that, say so and it should keep a cell — labelled as something other than a copy of its neighbour.

§ 3Vocabulary

Three ways to say "move down a list"

Four vertical lists, navigated identically, labelled three different ways — and two different words for the action that opens a row.

ScreenOpen action MoveBack
Choose a courseSelectUp / Down« Back
Round menu (in play)SelectUp / Down« Back
History (round list)SelectPrev / NextBack
History round menuSelectUp / DownBack
Player pickerOpenPrev / NextBack

Three of these are worth settling:

  • Prev / Next vs Up / Down. Both describe the same two buttons moving through the same vertical list. Up / Down matches the physical rocker and is what upstream uses; Prev / Next reads as horizontal.
  • Select vs Open. The player picker says Open where every other list says Select. Nothing distinguishes the two actions.
  • "« Back" vs "Back". Screens inheriting the upstream list footer show a guillemet; golf's own screens do not. These sit next to each other in the same flow — course selection into player count — so the change is visible.

Recommendation

Standardise on Up / Down for vertical lists, Select for opening a row, and one spelling of Back. Reserve Prev / Next for genuinely horizontal movement — the main menu tiles and the hole review, where it is already correct.

§ 4Duplicate keys

Golf redefines strings upstream already has

Three golf keys carry values identical to upstream keys already in the same file:

Golf keyUpstream keyValue
STR_GOLF_BUTTON_UPSTR_DIR_UP"Up"
STR_GOLF_BUTTON_DOWNSTR_DIR_DOWN"Down"
STR_GOLF_BUTTON_SELECTSTR_SELECT"Select"

Each duplicate is a second place a translator must find, and a second place these can drift apart. Since §3 settles on the upstream wording anyway, the golf copies can go.

STR_GOLF_BUTTON_BACK ("Back") is not a duplicate — upstream's STR_BACK is "« Back". Whichever spelling wins in §3, one of the two keys should then be retired rather than leaving both.

§ 5What holds up

Most of it is in good shape

Things I checked and found correct, so they are not on the list above:

  • Empty states are handled properly. History, the player picker and Trends each distinguish "no rounds yet" from "couldn't read your rounds" — the distinction that matters, since one means go play and the other means something is wrong.
  • The main menu is correct. Confirm reads Start on New round and Open on the review screens, and Prev/Next disappear when only one destination exists.
  • Statistics does not scroll, so its blank Up/Down cells are right rather than a missing hint.
  • The scorecard handles multiple players — it iterates enabled slots rather than assuming one.
  • Par-free courses are respected throughout — em dashes rather than zeroes, on the card, the totals band and the course list.

Suggested order

  1. §1, the round summary on finish. Highest value, smallest change, and independent of everything else.
  2. §2 and §3 together. Both are footer work; doing them in one pass means touching each screen's footer once.
  3. §4 with §3, since retiring the duplicate keys falls out of choosing the wording.

Findings are from reading the current main at 1987c52 — every mapLabels() call, every empty-state branch, and the post-finish routing. Hole-by-hole scoring was excluded at the owner's request.

Severity reflects what a user notices: §1 is a missing moment, §2 and §3 are visible on every screen, §4 is invisible until someone translates the app.
