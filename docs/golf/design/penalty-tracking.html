§ 1 What a press does

Confirm opens a two-option picker

Power already cycles the field, which frees Confirm entirely. Pressing it opens a sheet with two choices — Hazard or OB — and picking one does three things at once:

  • Adds one shot to the focused field. That is the swing you actually made.
  • Adds the penalty strokes. Hazard +1, OB +2.
  • Appends a marker — H or OB — beside that field's number.

Markers accumulate in order, so a hole that went badly reads H OB OB H and you can see the shape of it rather than just a count.

Why OB costs two

This matches USGA Local Rule E-5: two penalty strokes and a drop near where the ball went out, taken instead of stroke-and-distance. It is the standard local rule for casual play, and it is what most club cards assume — so the number on the device will match the number your group agrees on.

The stroke arithmetic

Total strokes gains a fourth term:

strokes = in100 + out100 + penaltyStrokes

penaltyStrokes = (hazards x 1) + (OBs x 2)

The three buckets stay disjoint and gain a fourth, which is an improvement on today: penalties currently hide inside the long-game count and quietly inflate it.

§ 3 Data model and storage

Ninety bytes, order preserved

A marker sequence needs order and it needs to know which field each event belongs to. Both fit in a nibble: 

static constexpr uint8_t MAX_PENALTIES_PER_HOLE = 8;

// One nibble per event, two events per byte, in the order they happened.
//   bits 0-1  field  (0 = putts, 1 = in100, 2 = out100)
//   bit  2    kind   (0 = hazard, 1 = OB)
//   bit  3    reserved
uint8_t penaltyCount[MAX_HOLES];                      // 18 bytes
uint8_t penaltyEvents[MAX_HOLES][MAX_PENALTIES_PER_HOLE / 2];  // 72 bytes

GolfRound grows from 164 to 254 bytes. On a device with ~380 KB of RAM that is not a consideration; the reason to pack at all is that the round travels through state.json on every flush, and a compact struct keeps that write small.

File format

Round files and state.json go to "v": 3, gaining a penalties array of [field, kind] pairs per hole. index.csv gains hazards and obs columns so trends stay a fold over the index with no round files opened.

v2 files are read, not rejected. Unlike the v1 → v2 break, nothing about the existing fields changes meaning — a v2 round simply has no penalties. Load it with zero penalties and upgrade on next write. Rejecting rounds already played would be gratuitous.

§ 4 Edge cases worth deciding now

Four things that will otherwise bite

1 · Undoing a mis-press

A wrong pick inflates the score, so removal has to exist. Down on a field with markers removes the most recent marker on that field, along with its shot and its penalty strokes — the exact inverse of adding it. Down on a field without markers behaves as it does today.

2 · The button lockout

Power only cycles the field when the short-power-button setting is not Sleep. If Confirm becomes the penalty button unconditionally and that setting is ever Sleep, there is no way to change fields at all and the scoring screen is stuck.

Rule: Confirm only becomes the penalty button while Power can cycle. When it cannot, Confirm keeps cycling the field and the picker moves to a long-press. The scoring screen must never reach a state with no field control.

3 · Advance still belongs to Power

Field on Scoring Zone advances to the next hole. That stays on Power. Confirm never advances — a penalty is not a reason to leave the hole, and you often add one mid-hole with shots still to come.

4 · Eight per hole is the cap

At the cap the picker still opens but reports that the hole is full rather than silently dropping the event. Eight penalties on one hole is already a story; nine does not need recording.

§ 5 Build order

Three milestones, host-testable first

Same split that has worked all project: everything verifiable without hardware goes first, the screens last.

Open question

The mock shows markers on the field where the shot happened. If a hole collects four or five, the focused row gets crowded at 480 px. The alternative is markers only in the PENALTY band and not beside the numbers — less immediate, but it never runs out of room. Worth deciding after you have seen four markers on one row on the actual panel.
