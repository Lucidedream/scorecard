A golf scorecard that runs on the reader

Three numbers per hole, seven buttons, no touchscreen, no network, no phone. This is the design for a scorecard app built as a fork of CrossPoint Reader, grounded in what the X4 hardware and the firmware actually give us.

§ 1 What we are building, and what we are not

The product in one paragraph

You keep score on the X4 while you play. Each hole records three numbers: total strokes, putts, and strokes played from inside 100 yards. When the round ends the device writes a file to the SD card and shows you the card. Everything happens on the device — no companion app, no cloud, no Wi-Fi needed on the course. To get rounds off, you either pull the SD card or use the webserver CrossPoint already ships.

StrokeInk is the reference point for the shape of the interaction — a dedicated e-ink card you press a button on instead of holding a phone — but this deliberately stops where their product starts adding a phone app, a course library of 20,000 courses, and an AI coach. The device half is the whole product here.

Out of scope, on purpose

  • Multi-player. One column of scores. No foursome axis on the scoring screen.
  • Fairways hit, GIR, penalties, sand saves. Not captured. Each one is another button press per hole on a device with no touchscreen.
  • Per-hole notes. Text entry through a 7-button keyboard grid mid-round is a feature nobody uses twice.
  • GPS, yardages to pin, shot tracking. The X4 has no GPS radio.

This will live in a fork

CrossPoint's SCOPE.md lists “Interactive Apps: No notepads, calculators, or games” under Out-of-Scope, and its Phase 1 roadmap has temporarily closed new network connectors while the team cuts RAM and flash. A golf scorecard is not going upstream, and it should not try to.

That is a normal outcome in this ecosystem — there are already a dozen public forks. It does mean the design has one hard structural requirement: keep the diff against upstream files down to a handful of lines so rebasing onto develop stays cheap. See §7.

§ 2 The three numbers, and why they are the right three

Every hole splits into three buckets

Strokes, putts, and strokes inside 100 yards are not three unrelated counters. If “inside 100” is defined as non-putt strokes played from inside 100 yards — wedges, pitches, chips, bunker shots — then the three numbers partition the hole completely and without overlap:

Long game
Inside 100
Putting
strokes − putts − in100
Tee shots and approaches
in100
Wedge and short game
putts
On the green

Long game is derived, never entered. That is what makes three counters worth more than three counters: after a round you can say “I lost six shots inside 100 yards,” which is the kind of sentence a paper card cannot produce. It also gives every stats screen in §5 an obvious spine.

The invariant

putts + in100 <= strokes, on every hole, at every moment. This is not a validation step at save time — it is enforced live by the counters, and it makes data entry faster rather than fussier:

  • Incrementing putts or inside 100 when the sum is already at strokes bumps strokes too. Adding a putt means you took a shot; the app knows that, so you don't press twice.
  • Decrementing strokes below the sum is refused, and the blocking field flashes inverse for one frame so you can see which number is holding the floor.

Confirm this reading

The whole model rests on “inside 100” excluding putts. If you meant it to include them — every shot from inside 100 yards, putts included — the buckets overlap, long game becomes strokes − in100, and putts becomes a sub-figure rather than a peer. Both are workable; they produce different screens. Say which you meant.

§ 3 Hardware facts that dictate the design

Six constraints, six consequences

These are read off the CrossPoint develop tree and the FreeInk SDK's XTEINK_X4 board profile, not from marketing copy. Each one forces a design decision rather than merely colouring one.

Constraint → consequence
What the hardware doesWhat the design has to do
~380 KB usable RAM, and ~47 KB of it is already the display framebuffer (100 × 480 bytes, single-buffer mode). The whole round lives in one fixed-size struct of about 170 bytes (§6). No std::vector, no std::string, no JSON document held open in the scoring path. Course and history lists stream from SD.
Seven buttons on an ADC ladder — Back, Confirm, Left, Right, Up, Down, Power — and no touchscreen. Touch is X4 Pro only; the X4 profile is NO_TOUCH. A focus-cycling entry model (§4). Every screen must be fully operable with Up/Down/Left/Right/Confirm/Back, and every target sized for reading, not tapping.
E-ink refresh is whole-buffer. The HAL exposes FULL_REFRESH, HALF_REFRESH, and FAST_REFRESH (custom LUT) — there is no windowed partial-update API to repaint just a numeral. One press = one full FAST_REFRESH. Hold-to-repeat has to coalesce, which CrossPoint's deferred requestUpdate() already does for free. A ghost-clearing pass gets scheduled on a cadence rather than on every paint (§4).
Sleep is a cold boot. Auto-sleep and the power-button hold both call esp_deep_sleep_start(); RAM does not survive. Wake re-runs BootActivity. Round state is written through to SD and reloaded on wake. The app resumes into the scoring screen at the current hole, not at a menu (§6).
No real-time clock. The X4 board profile is NO_SENSORS; the X3 is the sibling that carries a DS3231. HalClock::isAvailable() returns false, and syncFromNTP() needs Wi-Fi. The device cannot know the date on a golf course. Date is entered once at round setup, defaulted forward from the last round, with an optional NTP sync when Wi-Fi happens to be in range (§8).
Storage is generous. 16 MB flash with two 6.5 MB OTA app slots, plus the SD card. Flash budget is a non-issue. Rounds and courses are plain JSON on SD, human-readable and trivially exportable — no binary packing needed for space.

What CrossPoint hands us for free

This is the argument for building on CrossPoint rather than stock XTOS or bare metal: the hard parts are already written and field-tested.

  • ActivityManager — an activity stack with startActivityForResult() / setResult() / finish(), a single shared 8 KB render task, and a global RenderLock mutex. Screen navigation and lifetime management are solved problems.
  • UiListActivity / UiAppHost — list screens with viewport sync, button navigation, and hold-to-page already wired.
  • FreeInkUI components — table, metricCard, stepperRow, tabBar, progressBar, optionDialog, keyboard. The scorecard grid is a table; the stats screen is metricCards.
  • PersistableStore<T> — the JSON-on-SD persistence pattern CrossPointState itself uses. Round state inherits it directly.
  • The webserver — GET /api/files, GET /download?path=, POST /upload. Round export and course import need no new network code, which also keeps us clear of the closed area in SCOPE.md.
  • Deep sleep, battery, boot, crash handling, i18n, theming — untouched.
§ 4 The scoring screen and its input model

The screen you use 95% of the time

Everything else in the app is scaffolding around this one screen. It is shown here at 1:1 — 480 × 800 logical pixels, pure black on white, because that is literally what the panel can render.

Presses per hole is the metric that matters

A par-4 where you make 5 with two putts and one wedge is the median hole. Entering it costs 7 presses — Up, Confirm, Up Up, Confirm, Up, Right — which is about 130 presses over 18 holes. Without the pre-seed below it would be 11 presses and 200 for the round.

Pre-seeding to par

On first arrival at a hole, the strokes counter displays the hole's par in a lighter, outlined style. The hole stays flagged unentered until you touch any counter — so a hole you walked past without scoring stays blank on the card rather than silently recording a par. A par costs zero presses; a bogey costs one. It is a setting (Start hole at par), default on.

Refresh policy

  • Counter or hole change → deferred requestUpdate(), painted FAST_REFRESH. Deferred means multiple mutations in one loop pass produce one paint.
  • Every 8th paint → promote to HALF_REFRESH to clear accumulated ghosting, using promoteNextRefresh().
  • Entering the scorecard table → HALF_REFRESH. Dense grids are where ghosting is most visible.
  • Round summary on finish → FULL_REFRESH. It is a screen you look at once, and it deserves a clean plate.

Notes on the ones that aren't obvious

  • Golf Home shows Resume round as the first row only when /golf/state.json holds an open round — and in that case the app skips straight past it on wake (§6). The row exists for the case where you deliberately left the app.
  • Round Setup is a list of courses read from /golf/courses/. Picking one loads its pars and yardages. There is also Quick round, which assumes par 4 everywhere and lets you fix pars as you go — the escape hatch for playing somewhere you never entered.
  • History reads only /golf/rounds/index.csv, never the individual round files. Parsing a hundred JSON documents on a C3 to render a list would be visibly slow; a single CSV scan is not.
  • Course editor exists but is the last thing to build. Entering 18 pars and 18 yardages through stepperRows is tedious; dropping a JSON file onto the device over the existing webserver is not. Design for the upload path first.

Stats worth showing on Round Detail

All of these fall out of the three counters, and all are computable in a single pass over 18 small integers:

  • Score, score to par, front/back split
  • Putts total, putts per hole, count of 1-putts and 3-putts
  • Inside-100 shots total and per hole
  • The three-bucket breakdown — long / inside 100 / putting — as counts and as percentages
  • Worst holes by strokes over par
  • Across rounds: trend of score, putts, and bucket mix over the last n rounds
§ 6 Data model, files, persistence

170 bytes in RAM, JSON on the card

The in-memory round

struct GolfRound {
  static constexpr uint8_t MAX_HOLES = 18;

  char     courseName[40];
  char     tees[12];
  uint16_t dateYmd;               // (year-2000)<<9 | month<<5 | day
  uint8_t  holeCount;             // 9 or 18
  uint8_t  currentHole;           // 0-based

  uint8_t  par[MAX_HOLES];
  uint16_t yards[MAX_HOLES];
  uint8_t  strokes[MAX_HOLES];    // 0 == hole not entered
  uint8_t  putts[MAX_HOLES];
  uint8_t  in100[MAX_HOLES];
};                                // ~170 bytes, no heap

strokes[i] == 0 is the not-entered sentinel — a hole can never legitimately take zero strokes, so no parallel “entered” array is needed. Long game is never stored: long = strokes − putts − in100, computed at render time.

Write-through policy

Deep sleep destroys RAM, so an unwritten round is a lost round. But an SD write on every button press is both slow and pointless wear. The rule is a dirty flag plus four flush triggers:

When state.json is written
TriggerWhy
Hole changeThe natural commit point. One write per hole, ~18 per round.
5 s idle after a changeCovers standing on the tee after scoring the last hole.
onExit()Runs on both sleep paths — the auto-sleep timeout and the power-button hold both go through goToSleep(), which calls the activity's onExit(). This is the one that actually saves you.
Finish roundWrites the round file and appends the index row, then clears state.json.

Worst case — the battery is physically pulled mid-hole — you lose the counters for the hole you are standing on. That is an acceptable trade for 18 writes a round instead of 130.

Waking back into the round

Between holes the device sleeps. Waking is a cold boot through BootActivity, so the app must recognise an open round in state.json and go straight to the scoring screen at currentHole — not to CrossPoint's home, not to the golf menu. Press power, see your hole. Anything else makes the device feel worse than the pencil it replaces.

Sleep timeout wants a round-mode value

CrossPoint's default auto-sleep is tuned for a book you are holding. During an open round a longer timeout (10 minutes, say) trades a little battery for not cold-booting between the tee and the green. Given the X4 idles for weeks, spending some of that on a four-hour round is the right call — but it should be a setting, not a hardcode.

§ 7 Firmware architecture and fork hygiene

Keep the diff small enough to rebase forever

CrossPoint's develop branch moves fast and just refactored the entire activity system (the ActivityManager migration). A fork that scatters changes across upstream files will be unmergeable within two months. So: all new code in new directories, and exactly three upstream touchpoints.

New code

src/golf/
  GolfRound.h              the struct above
  GolfRoundStore.{h,cpp}   PersistableStore<GolfRoundStore> -> /golf/state.json
  CourseStore.{h,cpp}      enumerate + load /golf/courses/*.json
  RoundArchive.{h,cpp}     write round file, append index.csv, read index
  GolfStats.{h,cpp}        pure functions over GolfRound -> derived figures

src/activities/golf/
  GolfHomeActivity.{h,cpp}        : UiListActivity
  GolfSetupActivity.{h,cpp}       : UiListActivity  (course pick, then options)
  GolfScoringActivity.{h,cpp}     : Activity + UiAppHost   <- the one that matters
  GolfCardActivity.{h,cpp}        : Activity + UiAppHost   (tabBar + table)
  GolfHistoryActivity.{h,cpp}     : UiListActivity  (reads index.csv only)
  GolfRoundDetailActivity.{h,cpp} : Activity + UiAppHost   (metricCards + table)
  GolfCourseEditActivity.{h,cpp}  : UiListActivity  (stepperRows; built last)

GolfScoringActivity derives from Activity and uses UiAppHost directly rather than UiListActivity — it is not a list, and forcing it into the list protocol would fight the base class on every input.

Upstream files touched

FileLinesChange
src/activities/home/HomeActivity.cpp~4 One menu row, guarded by #if CROSSPOINT_GOLF.
platformio.ini~8 A [env:golf] that extends default and adds -DCROSSPOINT_GOLF=1.
src/activities/boot_sleep/BootActivity.cpp~6 Guarded check for an open round, routing straight to scoring on wake.

Three files, under twenty lines, all behind one build flag. Rebasing onto upstream develop becomes a routine operation rather than an archaeology project, and the same tree can still build a stock reader by selecting [env:default].

Rules inherited from CrossPoint's contributor guide

  • No xTaskCreate inside activities. Their guide says so explicitly, and nothing here needs a background task.
  • Mutations that render() reads must be under a RenderLock. loop() and render() run on different FreeRTOS tasks.
  • Never hold a RenderLock across a blocking call — including SD writes. Take the lock, mutate the counters, release, then flush.
  • Justify every heap allocation. The golf path should add well under 4 KB of heap; the round struct is static and the file I/O is streamed.
§ 8 Risks, and the date problem

What could make this unpleasant

RiskSeverityMitigation
No clock on the X4 High Enter the date once at round setup, defaulted to the last round's date and nudgeable with Up/Down — three or four presses. Optionally call syncFromNTP() when Wi-Fi is reachable at setup time and skip the prompt. The clock on the status bar is hidden entirely when no time is known, rather than showing a lie.
Refresh latency per press Medium Measure FAST_REFRESH on the actual panel in milestone M0 before committing to the input model. If it is slower than roughly 350 ms, hold-to-repeat becomes the primary entry mechanism and the pre-seed-to-par default matters even more.
Upstream API drift Medium The three-file touchpoint rule in §7. Rebase monthly rather than annually, so conflicts stay small.
Cold-boot wake feels slow Medium Round-mode sleep timeout (§6), plus resuming directly into the scoring screen so the boot lands somewhere useful.
Entering a course by hand is miserable Low Webserver upload is the primary path; a small script converts a scorecard into the course JSON. Quick round covers turning up somewhere unplanned.
Rain, gloves, cart vibration Low Nothing the firmware can fix — but it argues for large targets and for never requiring a precise or fast press. The X4 is not weather-sealed; that is a bag-and-pocket reality, not a software one.

Open questions

  1. Does “inside 100” include putts? §2 assumes not. This is the one answer that changes the data model.
  2. Portrait or landscape? The design above is portrait. If the device is going to sit in a cart holder, landscape 800 × 480 gives a much better scorecard table and a worse counter stack. GfxRenderer supports both.
  3. Nine-hole rounds and back-nine-only starts? Cheap to support at the data level, but it adds rows to Round Setup.
  4. What is it called? The app needs a name for the home menu row.
§ 9 Build order

Playable by M1

The sequence is chosen so the app becomes genuinely usable on a real round as early as possible, and everything after that is refinement of something you already carry.

M0

Skeleton and measurement

Fork, [env:golf] builds and flashes, home menu row appears, an empty GolfScoringActivity renders on the device. Measure real FAST_REFRESH and HALF_REFRESH timings and free heap. Those numbers validate or revise §4.

M1

The core loop — playable

GolfRound, the counters and their invariant, the scoring screen, quick-round setup, write-through persistence, and resume-into-round on wake. At the end of this milestone you can keep score for 18 holes and not lose it. Everything after is optional.

M2

Card, finish, history

The scorecard table with front/back tabs, finish-and-save writing the round file and index row, and the history list reading index.csv.

M3

Stats and export

Round detail with the three-bucket breakdown, cross-round trends, and CSV export retrievable over the existing webserver.

M4

Courses

Course library, JSON upload path with a conversion script, and the on-device course editor as the fallback.

M5

Whatever the first ten rounds ask for

Landscape mode, pace-of-play timing, nine-hole variants. Deliberately unspecified — playing with M1 for a month will produce a better list than guessing now.
