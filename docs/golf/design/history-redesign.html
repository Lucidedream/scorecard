§ 1What is wrong now

The card is a spreadsheet on a 480 px panel

The scorecard currently draws seven rows across ten columns — Hole, Par, Score, Putts, In 100, Out 100, Pen — which is seventy cells at roughly 17 px each. The round summary adds eight more rows on its own screen. Both are readable in the sense that the pixels resolve, and neither is scannable.

The scoring screen has never had this problem, and the reason is instructive: it shows one hole at a time, very large. That is the shape this device is good at. Review should borrow it rather than fight it.

Why a menu rather than more tabs

Tabs make sense when the views are peers you flick between while comparing. These are not: you open the card to scan, the holes to investigate one thing, the stats once at the end. A menu also gives Delete somewhere to live that is not a hidden gesture.

§ 4Deleting safely

Two files, one of them append-only

A round lives in two places: its JSON file under /golf/rounds/, and a row in index.csv. Deleting means removing both, and the index is the awkward one — it has been append-only by contract since the first milestone.

The order that cannot orphan data

  1. Rewrite the index first, to a staged index.csv.new with the row removed, verified by row count, then renamed through .bak — the same pattern the v2→v3 migration already uses and which is already tested.
  2. Then delete the round file.

This order matters. If the index rewrite fails, nothing is lost and the round is still listed. If the file deletion fails after the index succeeded, the worst case is an orphan JSON that nothing references — invisible, harmless, and reclaimable later. The reverse order risks a listed round whose file is gone, which is a broken entry the user can see and cannot fix.

The rule this bends

CONTRACTS §5.3 says index.csv is appended to and never rewritten wholesale. Delete cannot honour that literally. The concern behind the rule was losing rounds to a failed rewrite, and the staged-verify-rename sequence already answers it — the same reasoning that permitted the v3 migration.

What is deliberately not included

  • No undo. A confirm sheet that names the round is the protection. An undo buffer means keeping deleted rounds on disk, which is the opposite of what deleting is for.
  • No multi-select. Deleting rounds is rare; a checkbox mode on seven buttons costs more than it saves.
  • No editing. You asked for review without edit, and that is right: an archived round is a record. Correcting a mis-scored hole belongs in the round while it is still open.

One thing worth settling on the device

The card mock keeps Front 9 / Back 9 as tabs. With only three rows, both nines might fit on one screen as two stacked blocks — no tabs, no switching, the whole round visible at once. That is either much better or too tight, and the honest way to find out is to look at it on the panel rather than argue about it here.
