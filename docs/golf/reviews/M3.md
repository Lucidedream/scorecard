## The defect the worker found

§11 specified `(sum * 10 + n/2) / n` for one-decimal averages. That is only correct for
non-negative sums, and **average-to-par is signed**. C++ integer division truncates
toward zero, so a positive bias on a negative numerator rounds the wrong way:

| sum | n | true | §11 formula |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| −25 | 10 | −2.5 | **−2.4** |
| −7 | 2 | −3.5 | **−3.4** |

Verified independently before ruling. The failure direction is the bad one for a
scorecard: it flatters every under-par average, and nothing about `-2.4` on screen
announces that it should have been `-2.5`. This would have shipped silently.

Fourth genuine specification defect found by a worker, and the most subtle.

## Deviation accepted without a round-trip

My ruling was for **one** shared rounding helper. My reply arrived after the worker had
finished, so it shipped two: `averageTenths(uint32_t)` and `signedAverageTenths(int32_t)`.

Both round correctly, and the unsigned variant cannot receive a negative through its own
signature. The residual risk is narrow — a future signed figure passed to the unsigned
helper would implicitly convert and produce garbage. Not worth blocking a build the owner
wants to play a round with. **Fold the consolidation into M4.**

## Deliberately excluded

No handicap estimate. It needs a differential against course rating and slope, which no
course file carries. A number that looks like a handicap but is not one is the same class
of error as the fabricated course data in v2.1 — plausible, authoritative, and wrong.
